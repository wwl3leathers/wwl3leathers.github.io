Places

Twelve cities established

Twelve great cities form the surface roads of Talvaren. Each is shaped by its people, its calling, and the land that surrounds it.

Cindevale

Cindevale is a port city built during a period of ambitious reconstruction. Its roads, bridges, towers, homes, and several lighthouses are reinforced through practical magic rather than traditional masonry alone.

A large magical institution dominates the city's civic life, drawing wizards, researchers, apprentices, and skilled laborers from across the Stormlands. The surrounding lands suffer harsh storms, and lightning, flooding, and heavy winds regularly damage bridges, lighthouses, and other structures.

Cindevale's lighthouses guide ships through dangerous waters and serve as visible demonstrations of the city's magical infrastructure. The city is new, ambitious, and perpetually unfinished. In Cindevale, repair is not viewed as defeat but as part of the design.

Tharnhald

Tharnhald stands in one of Talvaren's coldest and most unforgiving regions. Bandit groups, hostile undead, and unknown creatures move through the frozen country with enough regularity that hardship has become part of ordinary life.

The city follows old northern traditions. Sturdy wood, stone, and thatch dominate its construction. An arena provides a constant test of skill, while bounty posts offer dangerous work to anyone brave—or foolish—enough to accept it.

Provisioners are treasured. Reliable food, drink, and supplies are the foundation of survival here. The fey have integrated themselves into Tharnhald society as record keepers, preserving bounties, lineages, debts, victories, and losses.

Sabenhal

Sabenhal is the crossroads of Talvaren. The surrounding region is heavily patrolled, and even the wild things of the world think twice before interfering with Caravaners' Guild traffic. Those that disrupt a caravan rarely do so twice.

The city is home to the largest Caravaners' Guild in the world. Goods from every known region flow through its markets, warehouses, roads, and departure yards.

The deep delvers operate the Great Lift and maintain a facility beside it. Stitchlings support nearly every part of the city's infrastructure, while fey scribes maintain trade records and secretly document rare objects recovered from below.

The Vast Below is regarded as both a dangerous mystery and a path to glory. Warriors arrive in Sabenhal seeking wealth, renown, or the chance to become the first name attached to an undiscovered place.

Mirekel

Mirekel lies among mixed marshlands, deep bogs, and verdant forests. The surrounding country is difficult to cross and crowded with arachni clusters. Rumors of a larger hive persist, though no reliable expedition has confirmed its location.

The undead have made the region their home. Their dwellings extend through the marshes, and at least one large underwater kingdom is believed to exist beneath the surrounding waters.

Undead from across Talvaren travel to Mirekel seeking healing from its necromancers and from the academy dedicated to finding a permanent cure for the undead affliction.

Mirekel itself is remarkably pristine. Its homes are beautifully maintained, its streets carefully tended, and its public spaces kept in immaculate condition. The undead appear to regard disorder as a personal insult.

Dangerous undead are rumored to serve as test subjects for procedures too uncertain to attempt on cooperative patients. One or two failures have escaped into the surrounding marshes.

There is no danger. Probably.

Corinvale

The desert hides what it decides needs to be hidden.

Corinvale is a nomadic therian city that follows a moving oasis through the desert. Every eight days, the tribes pack their homes, markets, workshops, and valuables before traveling to the next location: Saltwake, Red Dune Crossing, or Khelt's Rest.

Only one site functions as Corinvale at a time. The others remain dormant, retaining limited trade, a Caravaners' Guild outpost, and sealed or partially accessible structures.

Jewelers and thieves of every caliber call Corinvale and the surrounding desert home. Gems, jewels, precious fabrics, and treasures of every kind pass through its markets. Very unofficially confirmed rumors of a thieves' guild persist.

Corinvale is regarded as the richest city in the world. The city moves, the desert conceals, and the people who thrive there have learned that wealth is safest when no one knows exactly where it is.

Felenspire

The lands surrounding Felenspire are rich in cotton, game, leather, and other textile materials. The plains and rolling hills support abundant wildlife, making the region a reliable source of supplies.

They also contain spiders. And undead.

Arachni can be found throughout the countryside, creating constant demand for silk and making material-gathering dangerous work. The brave, well-prepared, or desperate can earn a living collecting resources in the surrounding hills.

Felenspire boasts an important library and a respected museum, including the persistent claim that the first stitchling ever crafted was created within the city.

Outfitters specializing in tailoring rarely lack for work here. It is the rare stitchling who feels comfortable sewing themselves back together.

Vinestrum

The woods surrounding Vinestrum are ancient. Some of the oldest trees in Talvaren are believed to grow there, though the forest has never been fully mapped and does not welcome every visitor.

Vinestrum's homes and establishments are not built from brick or stone. They are created through pact. Trees large enough to shelter entire dwellings rise throughout the forest, and bridges connect the canopy.

Persistent fey magic allows people to move between trees by paths that do not always obey ordinary distance. Wizards and priests travel to Vinestrum to commune with nature.

Fey throughout Talvaren are often secretive, but within Vinestrum they exist in something close to peace. A visitor who earns their trust might even be allowed to hear a secret or two.

At the center of the city stands a carefully maintained toadstool ring. When the proper time arrives, the fey gather there and return home.

For a while.

Verdaxi

Verdaxi is a port city that attracts adventurers from across the world. The surrounding jungle is corrupted by undead, arachni, bandits, and other dangers that have never been properly identified. The ocean is no safer.

The city is home to a grand monastery. Priests and warriors gather there to train, study, and prepare for the dangers beyond the walls.

At some unknown point in the past, a hole opened beneath the region and reached deep underground. Undead poured through it. After many lives were lost, the defenders achieved a brutal, hard-fought stalemate.

The tunnels leading to the rift have never been fully explored. The battle continues to this day.

It is the rare priest who earns the title of Paladin in Verdaxi. The name carries respect. Outfitters are prized here, because weapons and armor can never be permitted to run out.

Karth Hollow

Karth Hollow is a small manor city ruled by a duke and surrounded by quiet farmland. Its people live simply, relying on sturdy homes, practical crops, and the protection of old walls.

The town's most remarkable feature is the bridge leading to its gate. Nearly a kilometer long, it crosses an enormous chasm and is considered a marvel of engineering. A greenish glow rises from below, giving the crossing an eerie beauty while concealing whatever waits in the depths.

Caverns extend beneath the surrounding mountains. Some are natural, while others show signs of older construction. Undead, arachni, and bandits are common throughout the region.

Karth Hollow is modest, isolated, and practical. It is the sort of place where a farmer may live within sight of a duke's manor, where ancient secrets lie beneath ordinary roads, and where the bridge home is also a warning.

Veynhal

Veynhal stands within the old forests, where ancient growth surrounds the city and gives its institutions an atmosphere of age and continuity.

People from every known race and culture gather in Veynhal. Its libraries, academies, archives, workshops, and training halls attract those seeking knowledge rather than wealth or conquest.

Records are preserved here. Discoveries are debated, tested, and carried outward into the rest of Talvaren. Veynhal does not belong to one people or tradition. It is a place where different histories meet and where even dangerous knowledge may be studied under careful supervision.

The city is not peaceful because everyone agrees. It is peaceful because everyone understands what can be lost when knowledge disappears.

Stelavaren

Stelavaren rises among the mountains, where stone, ore, and difficult terrain have shaped every part of the city's character.

Humans and undead work side by side throughout its manufacturing yards. Laborers maintain the foundries, workshops, roads, and heavy machinery, while rogues are trusted with scouting, security, retrieval, and the movement of valuable materials.

The train yard is the heart of Stelavaren's expansion. Goods, equipment, and travelers pass through the city as rail lines extend into the mountain country and connect distant settlements.

The manufacturing yards remain active at all hours. Machines are built, tested, repaired, and improved as the city explores steam-powered devices and magitech.

Stelavaren is not a city of finished inventions. It is a city of working attempts. The mountains provide the materials. Its people provide the endurance.

And the trains keep moving.

Avenneris

Avenneris is an island city where warm air, open plains, and jungle greenery meet the sea.

The island offers some of the most sought-after homes in Talvaren. Gentle weather, extraordinary vistas, fertile ground, and long coastal views make its settlements desirable to merchants, travelers, and anyone with enough wealth to stop calling it temporary.

Avenneris maintains a powerful navy, though its waters are also frequented by pirates, smugglers, and adventurers pursuing rumors of lost treasure. Official patrols, questionable cargo, and honest commerce exist in close proximity.

Humans and therians share the island's homes, ports, markets, and competing ambitions. Rogues are drawn to its secrets and wealth, while necromancers serve communities shaped by shipwrecks, warfare, old graves, and the many dead claimed by the sea.

Avenneris offers warm weather, beautiful views, profitable danger, and a reasonable chance that the neighboring ship belongs to someone wanted by the navy.

It is, by nearly every measure, an excellent place to live—provided you know which ships to avoid.

The Vast Below

The Vast Below is not one of the twelve surface cities. It is an immense subterranean world beneath Talvaren, divided into layers of increasing danger and mystery.

Deep delvers maintain their own markets, routes, communities, and cities below the surface. The Great Lift and trolley systems provide sanctioned access to portions of this world, but much of the Vast Below remains unexplored.

Rare glimpses of the surface can be seen from its upper reaches. Between the layers, limited tunnels offer passage, while deeper regions keep their own dangers and secrets. The Vast Below is not merely beneath Talvaren.

It is another world waiting beneath it.

Twelve cities. One surface circuit. A world beneath the roads.

The Caravaners' Guild connects the great cities of the surface. The Vast Below follows its own routes, its own dangers, and its own history.
